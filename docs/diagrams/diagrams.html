Under the hood

Two engines feed every card

One looks closely at a single patient (is this drug a plausible cause?). The other looks across the whole population (where should the reviewer look first?).

Per case · Naranjo scoring

For every drug the patient was on when the problem started.
Q1
Known reaction? — search the openFDA label text
Q2
Started after the drug? — drug start vs. onset date
Q3
Better after stopping? — did the problem end after the course?
Q4
Back on restarting? — later course of same drug or class (RxNorm)
Q8
Similar reaction before? — history on related drugs, allergies
Q10
Objective evidence? — abnormal labs (ALT, AST…) near onset
≥ 9definite
5–8probable
1–4possible
≤ 0doubtful
Q5, Q6, Q7, Q9 need clinical judgement and are left to the reviewer on purpose.

Across the population · insights

Every patient, not just the ones in the deck.

Patient × (drug, condition) matrix

Yes/no per patient, IDF-weighted so common items count less
↓

PCA → k-means

2-D map of similar patients; k chosen by silhouette score (2–8); each group named by what sets it apart
↓

PRR signals (Evans criteria)

PRR = P(cond | on drug) ÷ P(cond | not on drug)
signal if n ≥ 3, PRR ≥ 2, χ² ≥ 4
Only conditions that start after the drug; a drug's own indication is never counted
↓

Priority 0–100

0.7 × strongest signal + 0.3 × how unusual for its group

Drugs ranked by score

Top drug = the deck the case goes into
